# Reader protection covers a medical cause of death that implies the method

Decided by the owner on 2026-09-02, in answer to a question the second Toka_ls rerun raised: the page
carried the source's closing paragraph, which the archive had omitted, "At 4 p.m., after resuscitation
failed to reverse hemorrhagic shock, Nonamev sadly passed away in Shanghai at the age of 26"
(2024 年 10 月 9 日下午 ... 因失血性休克抢救无效，于上海不幸逝世，终年26岁). The house rule kept a
suicide's method and any medication vague; a medical cause that implies the method sat at its edge.

## The decision

Keep the paragraph, drop the cause. The owner chose it over keeping the source's wording and over
dropping the paragraph as the archive had: the date, the place and the age are memorial facts nothing
protects a reader from, and the cause is the one clause that hints at the method.

## The rule

The house policy (`src/house-policy.ts`, the reader-protection bullet every sheet reads) now says: a
medical cause of death that implies the method (blood loss, a fall, a substance, an injury) is kept out
the same way as the method itself; "after resuscitation failed" or "passed away" says all a reader
needs, while the date, the place and the age stay.

## Consequences

- The Toka_ls page published at 15:52 UTC under `~/temp/agent/toka-rerun2-20260902` predates the rule
  and carries the cause; the next Toka_ls run applies the rule. The published tree is a runs-directory
  artifact, not the corpus, so nothing public carries it.
- A translator or judge that keeps the cause is now reporting against the rule, and a critic that
  reports its absence as an omission is wrong by the rule's own words.
