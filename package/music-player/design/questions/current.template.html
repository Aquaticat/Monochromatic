Tapping the folder chip opens the subdirectory picker. All three candidates reuse the settled picker presentation: D28 adaptive letter rail, D31 wrapped plain-text names at 48dp with primary plus 2dp indicator on the current folder, no segmentation, nothing truncated. Dark surfaces follow D41 and D42; the light picker surface follows the light decision below and is shown with L1.

K1 · recommended

Full-screen picker page

The cover swaps to a dedicated picker page: app bar with Navigate up plus Folders title, letter rail, and the wrapped names filling the panel.

  • Pros: maximum room for 70 to 100 names; reuses the unfolded picker component unchanged; no nested scroll regions for TalkBack or switch access.
  • Cons: hides the player while choosing; needs one back step to return.
K2

Dropdown panel under the chip

An exposed dropdown panel opens below the top row over the player, holding rail and names in a floating surface.

  • Pros: keeps deck and list context visible; matches the chip's dropdown affordance.
  • Cons: the panel competes with the player for a 923dp column; floating surface over content adds a second scroll region.
K3

Modal bottom sheet

A modal bottom sheet with drag handle and scrim rises over the player, holding rail and names.

  • Pros: thumb reach on the cover; standard Material modal semantics; scrim states modality without hiding the deck.
  • Cons: sheet height caps the visible names; scrim plus sheet adds two surfaces to maintain in both schemes.

Ranking: K1 > K3 > K2. K1 beats K3 because the full panel gives the name wall its settled room and keeps one scroll region per axis. K3 beats K2 because a modal sheet is the Material-blessed container for a long selection list, while a dropdown panel over the player leaves neither surface calm.

Decision: cover light surface

L1 · recommended

Ramp with a deck-seam hairline

Tonal ramp on a dimmed window: raised top row and deck in surfaceContainerLow, brightest list canvas, and one outlineVariant hairline where the deck meets the list.

  • Pros: tonal as Material intends, with a hairline exactly where tone alone fails; least ink for the separation achieved; mirrors the settled unfolded 1c treatment.
  • Cons: two separation mechanisms instead of one.
L2

Pure ramp, no hairlines

The same ramp with no hairline at any seam.

  • Pros: purest Material 3 reading; calmest surface; nothing extra to maintain.
  • Cons: the ramp steps sit within a few percent of each other; on a poor panel or at low brightness the deck may not read as separate.
L3

Flat surface with hairlines

One flat surfaceContainerLowest surface with outlineVariant hairlines at both the top-row and deck seams.

  • Pros: unambiguous at any brightness or panel quality; separation never depends on tone.
  • Cons: hairlines in an already high-contrast theme add noise; least Material-idiomatic.

Ranking: L1 > L3 > L2. L1 beats L3 because one hairline at the failing seam keeps the screen calmer than hairlines at every seam. L3 beats L2 because a guaranteed separation survives poor panels and low brightness, while L2's ramp-only steps can vanish on them.
