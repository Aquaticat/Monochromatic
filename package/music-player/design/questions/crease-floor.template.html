Pros

Cons

My read: Recommend the middle tested floor under this allocation for its visible separation without the first-screen browser reflow seen at P20. This is a design preference, not a claim that all content passes.

P0 · No independent floor beyond the crease

Pros

  • Preserves the selected A's current browser width and positive-result composition.
  • Does not introduce an additional spacing policy beyond keeping meaning-bearing marks off the actual crease.

Cons

  • A future narrower crease would reduce the effective total-gap requirement.
  • Offers no independent across-device separation guarantee; the captured 167px node-box proxy is not an ink minimum.

My read: Fallback if preserving current content width matters more than an independent minimum.

P20 · 20mm total minimum

Pros

  • Moves opposing information farther apart in both captured states while keeping the full deck and query header present.
  • Offers a stronger physical spacing policy for a narrower future crease.

Cons

  • Under this allocation, the narrower folder browser wraps earlier: Celldweller moves to another line and Clown Core falls below the initial viewport with the keyboard closed. Its scroll reachability was not tested.
  • No additional readability gain was established from this extra distance; untested names and keyboard states could incur further tradeoffs.

My read: Choose only if the stronger separation merits the visible browser-density loss, without replacing the browser or reducing deck targets.
